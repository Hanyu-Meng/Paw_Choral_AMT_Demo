PawCT: End-to-End Choral Transcription into Separate SATB Note Tracks

Hanyu Meng1, Zhanhong He2, Zixun Guo3, Yaolong Ju4

1UNSW · 2UWA · 3Queen Mary University of London · 4Great Bay University

Links: [ Paper | Code ]

Most choral transcription systems return one merged stream of notes. PawCT identifies which sections are singing and writes a separate MIDI track for soprano, alto, tenor, and bass.

From one choir recording to four usable parts

Performance at a glance

0.382 single-track note F1 +61.18% over the previous state of the art
0.225 average SATB note F1 +36.36% over the adapted choral baseline
0.313 zero-shot note F1 on Cantoría 2.72× the two-stage assignment baseline

Note F1 uses a 50 ms onset tolerance. Expand the tables below for the complete paper results; bold and underlined values mark the best and second-best results, respectively.

Model Overview

PawCT detects notes and assigns them to vocal parts in one model. A global training signal keeps the complete pitch content intact, while pitch range and melodic continuity help maintain consistent SATB lines.

Why can MuScriptor still look plausible?

A 35-second overview compresses a 50–100 ms onset shift to roughly one or two pixels. Note F1 requires an exact-pitch, one-to-one match whose onset falls inside the stated tolerance, so broadly correct pitch contours can coexist with low event scores. MuScriptor's rise from 0.037 at 50 ms to 0.240 at 100 ms is consistent with many timing near-misses; the remaining gap also permits missed, extra, split, or merged notes.

What does the comparison establish?

PagCT is task-specific, whereas MuScriptor-medium uses the released general-purpose checkpoint. This excerpt therefore illustrates task-aligned event localization versus out-of-domain generalization—not an architecture comparison under matched training. PagCT is substantially better under this event metric, but its 0.433 F1 also makes clear that the transcription is not error-free. The full YouChorale test-set results in the paper remain the primary quantitative evidence.

Error analysis

Why general-purpose transcription falls short on choir

MuScriptor-medium and PagCT achieve similar frame F1 (0.730 vs. 0.738), but PagCT obtains substantially higher 50-ms note F1 (0.382 vs. 0.084). This suggests that the main gap lies in localizing note events precisely rather than detecting sustained pitch activity.
