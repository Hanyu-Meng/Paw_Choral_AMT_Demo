PawCT: End-to-End Choral Transcription into Separate SATB Note Tracks

Hanyu Meng1, Zhanhong He2, Zixun Guo3, Yaolong Ju4

1UNSW · 2UWA · 3Queen Mary University of London · 4Great Bay University

Links: [ Paper | Code | Citation ]

Most choral transcription systems return one merged stream of notes. PawCT identifies which sections are singing and writes a separate MIDI track for soprano, alto, tenor, and bass.

From one choir recording to four usable parts

Abstract

Single-instrument automatic music transcription (AMT) has advanced substantially, yet a single instrument can contain functionally distinct musical parts that must be separated for practical use. This limitation is particularly consequential in choral music, where a part is a distinct vocal line sung by a voice section, conventionally soprano, alto, tenor, or bass (SATB). Existing choral AMT systems instead produce a single merged note track, limiting rehearsal, education, and score reconstruction. We introduce Part-aware Choral Transcription (PawCT), the first end-to-end framework to identify active SATB parts and transcribe each into a separate note-level MIDI track. PawCT combines part-specific prediction and presence gating with pitch-range and ordered-continuity priors and union-level supervision. On YouChorale, PawCT-OC achieves an average part-aware note F1 of 0.225, a 36.36% relative improvement over an adapted choral baseline. Its part-agnostic counterpart, PagCT, achieves a note F1 of 0.382, improving over the previous state of the art by 61.18%. These results suggest that jointly modeling transcription and part assignment is more effective than post-hoc assignment.

Performance at a glance

0.382 single-track note F1 +61.18% over the previous state of the art
0.225 average SATB note F1 +36.36% over the adapted choral baseline
0.313 zero-shot note F1 on Cantoría 2.72× the two-stage assignment baseline

Note F1 uses a 50 ms onset tolerance. Full per-part, frame-level, and zero-shot results are reported in the paper.

How PawCT keeps the parts separate

PawCT detects notes and assigns them to vocal parts in one model. A global training signal keeps the complete pitch content intact, while pitch range and melodic continuity help maintain consistent SATB lines.
